Station 2 · Activity 6.1 & Q9

Changes During Adolescence

Sort each change. Remember: the timing, nature and extent of these changes vary from person to person — every variation is normal.

Words to know

Puberty — the stage when the body changes, inside and outside, to become an adult capable of reproduction.
Secondary sexual characteristics — changes like voice, facial hair or breast development that help distinguish males and females but are not directly involved in reproduction.
Adam’s apple — the bump seen in the throat when the voice box grows, more in boys.
Acne — extra oily secretions can clog skin pores, causing pimples. Wash your face gently and regularly.

Did you know?

Adolescents, especially girls, may face blood-related problems from a lack of iron or vitamin B12. Spinach, kidney beans, raisins and figs give iron. Scientist Dorothy Hodgkin worked out the structure of vitamin B12 and won the Nobel Prize in 1964.

Station 5 · Reproductive health

The Menstrual Cycle & Myth-busting

The menstrual cycle is a natural process and a sign of good reproductive health. It usually recurs every 28–30 days, though anything from 21 to 35 days can be healthy. Menstruation lasts about 3 to 7 days.

Explore the cycle

Menstruation usually stops naturally around the age of 45–55.

Station 7

Check Yourself

Key ideas

Adolescence is the change from childhood to adulthood, roughly 10–19 years.
It brings physical, emotional and behavioural changes.
Hormones, released on signals from the brain, control these changes.
The menstrual cycle is natural; myths about it have no scientific basis.
A balanced diet, hygiene, exercise and a balanced social life keep adolescents healthy.
Say NO to tobacco, alcohol and drugs — the first time and every time.
