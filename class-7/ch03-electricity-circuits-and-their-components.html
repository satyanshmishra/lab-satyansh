Remember

A combination of two or more cells, with the + terminal of one touching the − terminal of the next, is called a battery. The thin glowing wire in an incandescent lamp is the filament.

Why it matters

Wires are made of copper (cheap and plentiful). Silver, copper and gold are the best conductors. Plastic, rubber and ceramic insulators cover wires, plugs and switches to protect us.

Caution — Our body is a conductor. Never touch switches with wet hands, and never experiment with the mains supply. Use only cells.

Key ideas

An electric cell is a portable source of electrical energy with + and − terminals.
A lamp glows only when there is a complete path (closed circuit).
In a battery, + of one cell joins − of the next.
An LED lets current pass in one direction only: long leg to +.
A switch completes or breaks a circuit, and can be placed anywhere in it.
Metals are conductors; plastic, rubber, glass and wood are insulators.
