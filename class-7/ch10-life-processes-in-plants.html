Station 2 · Activities 10.2, 10.3, 10.4

The Leaf Starch Test

Boil a leaf, decolourise it in alcohol (in a water bath), then add iodine. Blue-black means starch — food made by photosynthesis. Choose a leaf and run the test step by step.

Choose the leaf

Caution — Alcohol catches fire easily. Never put it directly near a flame; heat it in a water bath. Teacher demonstration only.

Stomata under the Microscope

A thin peel from the lower surface of a leaf shows tiny pores called stomata. They let carbon dioxide in and oxygen (and water vapour) out. Focus the microscope and switch between day and night.

Station 5 · Activity 10.7

Xylem & Phloem: The Plant’s Pipes

Place a white-flowered twig in red-ink water and watch it overnight. Then cut the stem. Xylem carries water and minerals up; phloem carries food from leaves to all parts.

Key ideas

Plants need sunlight and water to grow.
Leaves are the ‘food factories’; chlorophyll captures sunlight.
Photosynthesis needs CO₂, water, sunlight and chlorophyll; it gives glucose and oxygen.
Starch shows up blue-black with iodine.
Stomata exchange gases; xylem carries water up; phloem carries food.
All parts of a plant respire, day and night.
