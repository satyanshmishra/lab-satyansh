Methods of Separation in Everyday Life

From a farmer's field to your kitchen, mixtures are separated every day. Thresh and winnow grain, sieve flour, clean muddy water, get salt back from salt water and pull iron out with a magnet.

  1. Thresh and winnow
  2. Sieve the flour
  3. Clean muddy water
  4. Get the salt back
  5. Separate with a magnet
  6. Pick the right method
  7. Check what you learnt

From stalks to clean grain

First beat the wheat stalks to free the grains (threshing). Then pour the mixture from a height and let the wind do the work (winnowing).

ThreshingWinnowing

Tap “Beat the stalks” several times.

Shake the sieve

Flour has some bran and tiny stones mixed in. Drag the sieve left and right (or press the button) to shake it.

Drag the sieve sideways

Sieving is used when the parts of a mixture have different sizes.

Cleaning muddy water

Follow three steps: let the mud settle, pour off the water, then filter it.

Where did the salt go?

Salt dissolved in water cannot be filtered out. Heat the salt solution in a china dish and watch.

Turn up the flame.

Salt from the sea

Sea water is let into shallow salt pans. The Sun's heat evaporates the water, leaving salt behind. Have you seen white patches on dark clothes after they dry? That is salt from your sweat!

Nails in the sawdust

A carpenter's heap has iron nails mixed with sawdust. Drag the magnet over the heap, then drag it to the box to drop the nails.

Iron is a magnetic substance; sawdust is not.

Churning

Butter is separated from curd by churning. The lighter butter collects on top and can be taken out.
