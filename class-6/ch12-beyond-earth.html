Join the stars

Long ago, people imagined patterns in groups of stars. Tap one star, then another, to draw a line between them. Find every line of the pattern.

Today, the sky is divided into 88 constellations. Different cultures gave the same star groups different names and stories.

The star that stays still

Look at the northern sky. Move through the night and watch the stars. Then use the two pointer stars of the Big Dipper's cup to find the Pole Star.

Where did the stars go?

Light from streetlights, buildings and vehicles makes the night sky glow. Turn the city lights down and count the stars you can see.

India's first dark sky reserve is at Hanle in Ladakh. Lighting there is kept low so that the night sky stays dark and starry.

Planets don't twinkle muchStars twinkle a lot; planets shine more steadily. Venus is so bright it is called the Morning or Evening Star, though it is not a star.
Dhruva tārāThe Pole Star is known as Dhruva tārā in India. It is not visible from the southern hemisphere.
