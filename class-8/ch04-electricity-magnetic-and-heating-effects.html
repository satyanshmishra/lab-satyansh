Did you know?

Volta showed that two different metals with a salty or acidic liquid between them make electricity — leading to the first battery. Old batteries must never be thrown in regular garbage; their chemicals harm soil and water. Give them to e-waste collection centres.

Key ideas

A wire carrying electric current produces a magnetic field around it — the magnetic effect of current.
Reversing the current reverses the deflection of the compass needle.
A current-carrying coil acts as a magnet — an electromagnet. An iron core makes it much stronger.
More turns or more cells make an electromagnet stronger; it loses its magnetism when the current stops.
Current flowing through a wire produces heat — the heating effect, used in heaters, irons and kettles.
A cell produces electricity from chemical reactions between two different metals and an electrolyte.
